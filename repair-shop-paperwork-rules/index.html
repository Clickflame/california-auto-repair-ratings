California/Your Rights

What A California Repair Shop Must Give You

Five rules protect you every time you drop off a car in California. They come from the Bureau of Automotive Repair, and they take one minute to learn.

RWritten by Roger Diaz, Star Mobile Mechanics in Boron, California. Author Source

1

The Shop Must Be Registered

Auto shops in California must be registered with the Bureau of Automotive Repair. The Bureau is the state office that oversees repair shops and Smog Check stations.

2

You Get An Estimate First

The shop must give you an estimate for the job. Read it and ask about any line you do not follow before you say yes.

3

No Work Until You Approve

The shop cannot start work until you approve it. Your yes is what opens the job, so do not give it until the estimate makes sense to you.

4

You Can Ask For Your Old Parts

You can get your replaced parts back. The catch is timing. Ask before you approve the repairs, not when you pick up the car.

5

You Get An Itemized Invoice

When the job is done the shop must give you a final itemized invoice. Check it against the estimate you approved and keep both.

These five rules are from the Bureau of Automotive Repair auto repair page, read October 12, 2026.

A Simple Order To Follow

  1. Before you go, confirm the shop is registered with the Bureau.
  2. At the counter, get the estimate and read every line.
  3. Before you approve, ask for your old parts back if you want them.
  4. Then approve the work. Nothing should start before this step.
  5. At pickup, get the itemized invoice and compare it with the estimate.

Smog Check Basics

Gas, hybrid and alternative fuel vehicles from 1976 and newer need a smog check every two years when the DMV renewal notice asks for one. So do diesel vehicles from 1998 and newer. Gas and hybrid vehicles 8 model years old or newer pay a smog abatement fee instead, and electric vehicles are exempt.

A smog check is also needed when most vehicles are sold and when a vehicle from another state is first registered in California. These points are from the Bureau of Automotive Repair Smog Check page, read October 12, 2026.

Registrations In Our Shop Count

The state publishes its list of repair licenses each month through the Department of Consumer Affairs public information page. A shop that trades under a different name than the one on its license will not match, so always look a shop up with the Bureau yourself.
